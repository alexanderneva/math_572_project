\section{Solving Model 1}
Following \cite[Chap. 7~]{boyce2013elementary} to solve \ref{model1} we look for a fundamental solution set given by the eigenvectors of the matrix 
\begin{equation}
  M := \begin{bmatrix}
    0 & a \\
    b & 0 \\
  \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The characteristic polynomial of $M$ is 
\begin{eqnarray}
  p(\lambda) &= \lambda^2 - \text{Tr} M \lambda + \det{M}\\
	     &= \lambda^2 - ab
\end{eqnarray}
Setting $p=0$ and solving for $\lambda$ yields
\begin{align}
  \lambda_+ = \sqrt{ab},\quad \lambda_- = -\sqrt{ab}.
\end{align}
These will be real valued when $\text{sign}(a)=\text{sign}(b)$ and a $\C$-conjugate pair otherwise. The eigenvector $e_+$ comes from
\begin{align*}
  \lambda_+ I - M &= 
    \begin{bmatrix}
      \sqrt{ab} & -a \\
      -b  & \sqrt{ab}
    \end{bmatrix} & \\
   &= \begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{ab}&a\\0&0\end{bmatrix} & \left(\frac{-b}{\sqrt{ab}}R_1 \to R_2\right)\\
\end{align*}
Hence, $v_+ = (1,-b/\sqrt{ab})$ is an eigenvector for matrix $M$. Similarly, we can find $v_- = (a/\sqrt{ab},1)$. The Wronskian of these vectors is 
\begin{align*}
  W(t)&=\begin{vmatrix}v_1& v_2\end{vmatrix} \\
      &= \begin{vmatrix}e^{\sqrt{ab}t} & \frac{ae^{-\sqrt{ab}t}}{\sqrt{ab}}\\ 
	\frac{-be^{\sqrt{ab}t}}{\sqrt{ab}} & e^{-\sqrt{ab}t} \\
      \end{vmatrix}\\
      & = 1 + \sqrt{ab} \neq 0\quad \forall a,b\in\R
\end{align*}A general solution for the IVP \ref{model1} is 
\begin{equation}
  \phi(t) = c_1e^{\sqrt{ab}t}e_+ + c_2 e^{-\sqrt{ab}t}e_-
\end{equation}
